Is skin picking self-harm? Is it vanity?

Updated November 2026

No to both. These are the two things people with dermatillomania hear most, and they both get it wrong.

It isn't self-harm

Self-harm is done to hurt the body, often to drown out emotional pain. Picking has a different goal. For most people it soothes, relieves tension, or aims at something: a smooth surface, a scab gone. The damage is a side effect, not the point. The TLC Foundation for BFRBs says it plainly: BFRBs are not self-harm.

The difference matters in practice. Treatment for picking works on the habit and its triggers, not on an urge to hurt yourself. If you do have thoughts of hurting yourself, that's a separate thing worth telling a doctor about.

It isn't vanity

Many people pick at spots or bumps that bother them, and some pick at skin that looks fine but feels uneven. That can look like a beauty routine from outside. It isn't one: people pick even though it makes their skin look worse, they try to stop, and they can't. That last part, repeated tries to stop, is in the diagnosis itself.

So what drives it?

There's no single cause. What researchers point to:

  • Feelings. Stress, anxiety, boredom, tiredness. Picking calms them for a moment, and that relief is what keeps the habit going.
  • Sensation. A bump or rough patch that feels wrong and pulls the fingers to it.
  • Habit. Some picking happens with no feeling at all, on autopilot, while reading or on the phone.
  • Biology. BFRBs run in families, and are thought to come from a mix of genes, brain and environment.

Afterwards, guilt and shame are common, and they add to the stress that sets off the next round. More in why do I pick my skin?
