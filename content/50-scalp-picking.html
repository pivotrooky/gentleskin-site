Scalp picking

Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

Scalp picking is picking at bumps, scabs, flakes or rough spots on your scalp, often without noticing, and finding it hard to stop. It's one of the most common forms of skin picking (dermatillomania), and one of the easiest to hide, which is part of why it can go on for years.

Why the scalp?

  • It's always within reach. Your hand rests near your head while you read, work, scroll or think.
  • You can't see it. Picking by touch means searching for anything uneven, and there's always something to find.
  • It hides well. Hair covers the marks, so there's less to make you stop.
  • The skin gives you material. Dandruff, dry patches, folliculitis or a healing scab all invite more picking, and picking makes more scabs.

What keeps it going

Like other skin picking, scalp picking usually does a job: it calms you down, keeps you focused, or fills a quiet moment. Finding and removing a flake or scab brings a small hit of satisfaction. That relief is real, which is why willpower alone rarely works. The goal is to make it harder to start and to give the same job to something else.

Things to try

Notice your situations

Most scalp picking happens in a few situations: at the desk, on the couch with a phone, in bed, while reading. For a week, note when your hand goes to your head. Once you know your top two or three, that's where the changes below go.

Put something in the way

  • Wear a hat, a headband, or your hair up during your high-risk times.
  • Keep nails short and smooth so there's less to catch with.
  • Use a fingertip bandage or a ring on the finger you search with; the change in feel is a reminder.

Give your hands a different job

Keep a fidget, a smooth stone or a stress ball wherever you usually pick. When you catch your hand going up, move it to the object instead. Holding a pen, a mug or your phone with both hands works too.

Look after your scalp

If flakes or itchy bumps set you off, treating them takes away triggers. A dermatologist or pharmacist can suggest the right shampoo for dandruff or a dry, itchy scalp. Ask a doctor about any spot that's sore, swollen or not healing.

Ride out the urge

When the urge to search comes, try urge surfing: notice it, breathe, and watch it peak and pass, usually within a few minutes.

Be kind about slips

You'll catch your hand in your hair again. That's normal and doesn't erase progress. What matters is the trend over weeks, and every time you catch it is practice. If it's causing wounds or taking a lot of your time, a therapist who knows body-focused repetitive behaviors can help; the TLC Foundation for BFRBs lists them at bfrb.org.
