How to find help for skin picking

Updated November 2026

You don't need to be at your worst to ask for help. If picking hurts your skin, takes your time, or makes you hide, that's enough.

Therapist directories

  • The TLC Foundation for BFRBs keeps a directory of medical and therapeutic providers. Listings marked as graduates of its training institute have done TLC's 13-hour clinical program on BFRBs. Many offer online sessions.
  • The International OCD Foundation has a search for therapists, clinics and support groups, in person and online. Skin picking is one of the OCD-related disorders it covers.

Both directories are self-listed: the organisations don't endorse individual therapists, so ask questions.

What to ask a therapist

  • Have you treated skin picking or other BFRBs before?
  • Do you use habit reversal training or CBT for it?
  • Is it in person, online, or both?

If you can't find a BFRB specialist, a therapist trained in CBT is a good second choice.

Your doctor

A GP or family doctor can look at your skin, treat infected wounds, and refer you on. In the UK, the NHS says a GP may refer you to a specialist for talking therapy, and to a dermatologist if the skin is badly damaged. You can also refer yourself to NHS Talking Therapies in England.

Bring something to the appointment

A few weeks of notes on when, where and how long you pick makes the first session easier. See tracking skin picking.
