Skin picking at night

Updated November 2026

For some people the worst time is the end of the day. The house is quiet, you're tired, and you're alone with a mirror or a phone. Picking at night is the same skin picking as any other time, and the same things help. They just need setting up for the places where it happens.

Why night

You're tired. Late at night you have less in reserve to notice your hand moving and stop it.

The bathroom mirror. Washing your face or brushing your teeth puts you close to a mirror, often in bright light, with time alone. That's where focused picking tends to start: one spot, then another.

In bed. Scrolling or lying awake, your hand drifts to your face, scalp, arms or lips without you noticing. That's automatic picking.

The day catches up. The NHS lists boredom, stress and anxiety among the things that set off picking, and the end of a day can bring all three. More in why do I pick my skin?

What research says about sleep

In a study of 170 adults with skin picking disorder, 234 with hair pulling disorder and 146 without either, both groups with a BFRB reported more sleep complaints than the unaffected adults. A second study of 87 adults found poorer sleep quality in people with hair pulling or skin picking disorder than in controls, but poor sleep went with worse hair pulling, not with worse skin picking. A study of children found more sleep disturbance in those with BFRB symptoms.

So sleep and BFRBs are linked, but these studies don't show which causes which, and none shows that sleeping better reduces picking. If you sleep badly, it's still worth raising with a doctor for its own sake.

What people try

At the mirror:

  • Use dim light at night and put the magnifying mirror away.
  • Set a timer before you go in, and leave when it rings.
  • Keep tweezers and pins somewhere inconvenient. The NHS suggests not keeping them where you can easily get at them.
  • Put on moisturiser when the urge comes, another NHS suggestion.

In bed:

  • Wear thin cotton gloves or plasters on the fingertips you pick with. The NHS suggests gloves, and short nails.
  • Keep something to hold by the bed, like a soft ball or a fidget.
  • Charge your phone away from the bed, so scrolling in bed doesn't happen.
  • If an urge comes, wait it out with urge surfing instead of fighting it.

Write down when you pick for a week or two. It shows whether night really is your worst time, and whether it's the mirror, the bed or both. Tracking skin picking has more on what to note.

Getting help

If picking is causing wounds or scars or keeping you up, a therapist who treats BFRBs can help you work out a plan for those hours. See how to find help.
