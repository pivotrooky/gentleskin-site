BFRBs: the family skin picking belongs to

Updated November 2026

BFRB stands for body-focused repetitive behavior. It's the name for a group of behaviors where a person repeatedly picks, pulls, bites or scrapes their own skin, hair, lips, cheeks or nails, it damages the body, and they've tried more than once to stop or cut down.

The members

The two best known have their own diagnosis in the DSM-5-TR, the manual psychiatrists use: skin picking disorder (excoriation, or dermatillomania) and hair pulling disorder (trichotillomania). Others include nail biting, cheek and lip biting, and cuticle picking. Many people have more than one.

Where they sit

The DSM-5-TR lists skin picking and hair pulling under obsessive-compulsive and related disorders, next to OCD. That doesn't mean a BFRB is OCD. Picking usually isn't driven by an obsession; it tends to relieve tension or feel satisfying for a moment.

What's known about them

  • They're common and under-diagnosed. Skin picking alone affects about 2 to 3 in 100 people at any given time.
  • They run in families more than chance would predict.
  • They affect men and women. More women come forward for treatment, but men may simply report it less.
  • They aren't caused by bad parenting and aren't self-harm.

The treatment with the most evidence is the same across the family: behavioral therapy built around habit reversal. More in treatments that have evidence.
