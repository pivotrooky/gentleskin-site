Why track your skin picking

Updated October 2026 · 4 min read

Tracking skin picking turns a blur into a picture: when it happens, where on your body, how aware you were, and whether it's getting better. Noticing is the first step of habit reversal training, the most-used therapy for skin picking, and a log is the simplest way to practice it.

What tracking shows you

  • Your situations. Most people pick in a handful of places and times. Seeing them is what lets you change them.
  • Automatic versus focused picking. Some picking happens without noticing, some is deliberate, at the mirror. They need different fixes.
  • Progress you'd otherwise miss. A bad day feels like failure. A chart of the month often shows the trend going the right way.
  • What helps. When you try a change, like gloves at night or a fidget at the desk, the log shows whether it worked.

What to log

Keep it light, or you'll stop doing it:

  • Where (face, scalp, lips, cuticles, or your own spots)
  • How many, or how long, whichever fits how you pick
  • How aware you were
  • Optionally: the urges you rode out without picking

Log as soon as you can after it happens. A note at the end of the day is better than nothing, but memory blurs fast.

Keep it shame-free

A log is information, not a report card. Streak counters that reset to zero after one slip can make a hard day worse, so skip them. Watch the trend over weeks, set a daily target you can reach, and count the urges you got through, not only the picks.

What to bring to therapy

If you see a therapist, a few weeks of tracking makes sessions much more useful. Bring:

  • Totals per day or week since your last session
  • Which body parts, and what time of day
  • How aware you were, on average
  • What you tried, and what seemed to help

Your therapist sees the pattern in minutes instead of you reconstructing it from memory.
