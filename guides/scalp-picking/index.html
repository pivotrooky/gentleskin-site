Scalp picking

Updated October 2026

Scalp picking means picking at bumps, scabs, flakes or rough spots on your scalp, often without noticing. It's a common form of skin picking (dermatillomania). Because hair covers the marks, it can go on for years without anyone knowing.

Why the scalp

Your hand is close to your head whenever you read, work or think, so it's easy to reach. You can't see your scalp, so you pick by feel, searching for anything uneven, and there's nearly always something to find. Dandruff, dry patches, folliculitis and healing scabs give you more to pick at, and picking leaves more scabs.

What keeps it going

Like other skin picking, it usually relieves something for a moment. Finding and removing a flake or a scab feels satisfying, and the habit grows from that. This is why cutting down usually takes changing your situation, not only deciding to stop.

What people try

Start by noticing when your hand goes to your head. For most people it happens in a few places, like the desk, the sofa with a phone, or bed.

In those places, put something in the way. A hat, a headband or tied-up hair makes the scalp harder to reach. Short nails help. Some people wear a plaster or a ring on the finger they search with, so the different feel reminds them.

Keep a fidget or a smooth stone where you usually pick, and move your hand to it when you catch it going up. Holding a mug or a pen with both hands works too.

If flakes or itchy bumps set you off, ask a pharmacist or a dermatologist about the right shampoo. See a doctor about a spot that's sore, swollen or not healing.

When the urge to search comes, urge surfing can help you wait it out.

Slips

You'll find your hand in your hair again, and that's expected. Catching it is part of the practice. If it's causing wounds or taking a lot of your time, a therapist who treats BFRBs can help. The TLC Foundation for BFRBs lists them at bfrb.org.
