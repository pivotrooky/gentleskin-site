Urge surfing

Updated October 2026 · 4 min read

Urge surfing means riding out an urge, like the urge to pick your skin, by watching it rise, peak and fall instead of acting on it or fighting it. The idea comes from psychologist Alan Marlatt's work on relapse prevention, and it's used today for all kinds of urges, from cravings to body-focused repetitive behaviors like skin picking.

Why it works

An urge feels like it will keep getting stronger until you give in. It won't. Urges behave like waves: they build, crest, and fade on their own, usually within minutes. Every time you pick, your brain learns that picking is what ends the urge. Every time you ride one out, it learns the urge ends anyway. That second lesson is what makes the next urge a little weaker.

Fighting an urge ("don't pick, don't pick") tends to keep your attention locked on it. Surfing takes a step back: you notice it with curiosity, the way you'd watch weather pass.

How to do it, in about a minute

  1. Notice. Name it: "I'm having the urge to pick." Catching it is already a win.
  2. Find it in your body. Where do you feel it? Tingling fingertips, a tight jaw, restlessness, a pull toward the mirror.
  3. Breathe with it. Slow breaths, in and out. Picture the urge as a wave and your breath as the board.
  4. Watch it change. Notice it get stronger, then peak, then start to soften. You don't have to make it go away.
  5. Let it pass. When it has eased, give your hands something else to do and move on.

Some urges pass in seconds, some come back in sets. Each one you ride out is practice.

Tips that make it easier

  • Keep your hands busy while you surf: closed fists, a stress ball, a fidget.
  • Leave the trigger: step away from the mirror, put the phone down, stand up.
  • Count the wins. Logging urges you got through shows progress that a count of picks alone hides.
