Focused and automatic picking

Updated November 2026

Researchers split skin picking into two styles. Most people do some of both, but knowing which one is yours changes what helps.

Focused picking

You know you're doing it. Often at a mirror, sometimes with tweezers or good light, hunting a particular spot or scab, sometimes for a long time. It usually follows a feeling, like tension, an urge, or a spot that has to go, and brings relief or satisfaction.

Automatic picking

You don't notice until afterwards. Your hand goes to your face or scalp while you read, scroll, drive or lie in bed, and you only see it when there's blood or a mark. The MSD Manual puts it as picking "without full awareness".

Where the split comes from

In 2009, researchers at the University of Wisconsin-Milwaukee built a questionnaire for adult skin picking, the Milwaukee Inventory for the Dimensions of Adult Skin Picking (MIDAS). When they analysed the answers, two separate scales came out: one for focused picking and one for automatic picking.

Why it matters

Habit reversal, the main therapy, starts with awareness: noticing the picking, and what comes just before it.

  • If you pick automatically, the work is mostly noticing. Keeping your hands busy in the places it happens (phone, bed, desk), gloves or plasters on the fingers, and short nails all put a bump in the road before the pick.
  • If you pick on purpose, the work is mostly the urge and the setting. Waiting out the urge, covering or avoiding the mirror, keeping tweezers out of reach, and doing something else with your hands.

Writing down where and when it happens is how most people find out which style is theirs. See tracking skin picking and urge surfing.
